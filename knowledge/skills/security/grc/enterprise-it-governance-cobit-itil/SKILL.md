# Enterprise IT Governance (COBIT and ITIL)

This skill guides the AI to govern IT as a business capability, distinct from securing it. COBIT and ITIL answer "are we running IT well and in line with strategy?", while security frameworks answer "are we protected?".

---

## 🏛️ 1. COBIT 2019

- Distinguishes **governance** (board-level: Evaluate, Direct, Monitor) from **management** (plan, build, run, monitor).
- Defines governance and management objectives across domains such as APO (Align, Plan, Organize), BAI (Build, Acquire, Implement), DSS (Deliver, Service, Support) and MEA (Monitor, Evaluate, Assess).
- **Design factors** (enterprise strategy, risk profile, size, threat landscape, compliance) tailor the governance system.
- COBIT's governance layer is where IT risk appetite is set; security frameworks then implement within it.

---

## 🔄 2. ITIL 4

- The **Service Value System** describes how demand and opportunity become value through the service value chain.
- **Practices** (for example, incident management, change enablement, service continuity) replace the older process language.
- ITIL gives operational vocabulary for the security program: incident, problem, change and continuity are shared terms.

---

## 🔗 3. How the Layers Fit

- **Board/EDM** (COBIT): sets direction, risk appetite and accountability.
- **Management** (COBIT + ITIL practices): operates the services and the controls.
- **Security frameworks** (ISO 27001, NIST CSF, CIS): specify the controls.
- **Assurance** (SOC 2, ISO certification, FedRAMP): provides external evidence.

Name the layer explicitly in any governance discussion to avoid conflating board accountability with operational detail.
