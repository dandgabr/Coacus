the [ANPD Digital ECA hub](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/eca-digital/)

## Scope and controls

Assess targeted products first. For other products, document the combined
likely-access criteria in Article 1 and the ANPD guide: attractiveness and
probable use, ease of access and significant risk to privacy, safety or
development. The guide identifies categories with presumed likely access,
including social networks, online games, general-purpose generative AI and
marketplaces offering age-restricted products, subject to the specific
category and facts. Evaluate any rebuttal on evidence. Inspect how chat or
sharing works and the product's predominant function before assigning a
social-network role. Consider role-specific rules for devices, operating
systems, stores, application providers and browser-accessible services.

Separate general age assurance from reliable age verification for the
inappropriate, inadequate or prohibited access covered by Article 9(1).
Check Articles 10–15 and Decree Articles 24–27 for the applicable method,
signal and own-control duties. For the app-store or operating-system signal
covered by Decree Article 25, restrict it to what is needed for the age
threshold. Exclude exact birth date, civil identity and profiling data from
that signal. Use the more protective result where the Decree applies to
divergent age information. Support challenge and correction. Keep age data
purpose-bound and define expiry and access restrictions for permitted age
information. Under Decree Article 24(3), if documents are collected, limit
processing to the necessary age or age-band information. Delete the captured
document image, copy and other document information immediately and
irreversibly after extracting what is needed. Check for unintended copies
in temporary storage and downstream systems. Under Digital ECA Article 14,
keep the provider's own mechanisms against inappropriate access independent
of the measures adopted by the store or operating system.

Apply protective defaults, safeguards against compulsive use, supervision and
reporting mechanisms where the law requires them. Check Article 17 for visible
supervision, controls over contact, time, recommendations and geolocation.
Assess user-facing safety information and the progressive autonomy of children
and adolescents. For Article 24, assess the
social-network context and apply the guardian link for covered accounts up to
and including age 16. Check limits on advertising profiling and age-related
data use under Articles 22 and 26. Consider game interaction defaults and
other feature-specific rules under Articles 20–21. Check representative and
Portuguese-language communication requirements where applicable.

For covered products, assess risks to the safety and health of children and
adolescents under Decree Article 47. Record Article 16 duties for the controller
when it processes their data, including risk mapping and preparation of the
report for an authority request. Record risk likelihood,
severity, mitigations and ongoing effectiveness and prepare the accessible
public summary required by the decree. Consider privacy, interactions,
recommendation, advertising and compulsive use as applicable. Separately apply
the Article 31 Portuguese semiannual transparency report when the covered
application has more than one million registered children or adolescents with
internet connection in Brazil. Record the method, date and uncertainty of the
population estimate.

## NIDUS registration and reporting

1. **Current Article 27 duty.** Check the facts, Brazil scope and provider role.
   Identify the authorized owner and current communication, removal and
   preservation process. Check the narrow licensed or
   editorial-content exceptions in MJSP Ordinance Article 1(4) under their
   statutory conditions. The foreign clearinghouse exception in Decree Article
   37(2) and MJSP Ordinance Article 6(2) requires a foreign report submitted
   under a legal duty, identical reporting of the same facts and availability
   to Brazilian authorities. Assess uncovered facts and registration separately. Use a restricted incident record and
   keep sensitive content out of ordinary project tools.
2. **First stage.** DG/PF Ordinance 19.107/2026 was published on 20 October
   2026. Start the 60-day registration review from publication under MJSP
   Ordinance Article 35(2). Check the applicable provider categories and
   whether reportable facts were detected since 17 March 2026. Consider
   the adult-content and sexual-meeting providers specified by MJSP Ordinance
   Article 1(5),
   alongside targeted or likely-access products. The Federal Police portal
   distinguishes mandatory registration, recommended registration for
   covered risk without detection, and voluntary registration for other
   providers. Register immediately upon first meeting the mandatory conditions
   after the initial filing window, as explained by the MJSP bulletin. Record
   the basis, authorized representative, institutional and technical
   contacts, complete submission receipt and review status. Use the
   [CNCA technical registration guide](https://www.gov.br/cnca/pt-br/arquivos/nidus_guia_tecnico_cadastro_credenciamento_provedores_vfinal.pdf)
   to verify the submission procedure.
3. **Second stage.** The CNCA implementation material still described a
   separate Federal Police activation act as pending at the review date.
   Recheck the DOU and CNCA before relying on that status. Article 35(3)–(4)
   provides up to 90 days after the first-stage registration period ends for
   the second act and up to 120 days from that act's publication for the
   second-stage compliance deadline. Once the reporting clock applies,
   Article 8 counts continuously from confirmed detection. Verify the
   category: 12 hours for credible, imminent or ongoing risk; 72 hours for
   the specified cases outside the first category; seven days for other
   covered cases. Keep Article 27 duties active through the transition.
4. **Evidence.** In a readiness review, use a synthetic incident ID, decision,
   time and timezone, owner, applicable act, channel, receipt placeholder
   and escalation status. For a real case, use authorized official channels
   and the protected evidence process. A personal data security incident may
   separately require ANPD and affected-person notification under the LGPD.
