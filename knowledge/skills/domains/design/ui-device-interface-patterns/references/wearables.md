# Wrist-Worn Interfaces

## Context

Design around a brief glance, a short primary task, and a small usable region. Account for a round or rectangular display, interruptions, ambient visibility, and the potential presence of a crown, bezel, or side controls.

## Controls

Place the primary status and the next action where they survive truncation and round edges. Follow the platform's rotary behavior for supported scrolling or selection and expose reachable touch or assistive equivalents. Hand a lengthy edit to a companion screen with the user's context and a clear handoff state.

## States

Make paused, running, completed, and failed actions distinguishable at a glance. Preserve the task through wrist lowering or app suspension. Make start and stop actions unambiguous and use haptics as optional reinforcement.

## Accessibility

Check large text, focus and speech labels, contrast in bright surroundings, and the smallest supported display. Avoid communicating essential status through sound, color, or vibration alone. Keep incidental contact from activating an irreversible action.

## Evidence

Samsung's Gear draft discusses glanceability and the rotating bezel in its design principles and interaction chapters. See the [archive map](aresluna-source-map.md), [Wear OS principles](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/principles), and [Apple watchOS guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos).

## Validation

Start and pause a timer, lower the wrist, resume the task, and recover from an interrupted action. Verify on the smallest supported shape with enlarged text and with and without rotary input.
