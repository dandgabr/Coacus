# Shared Touch Tabletops

## Context

Design for multiple people standing or sitting around one surface, with different reading orientations, reaches, and simultaneous touches. Distinguish local workspace actions from changes that affect the shared session.

## Controls

Expose rotate, move, and confirm actions within reachable regions. Let people orient movable content locally. For ordinary repositioning and rotation, provide tap controls to select the object, choose a destination or angle, and confirm. Keep them usable from the participant's side. Treat contact identifiers as gesture inputs rather than reliable identities.

## States

Show which object is being manipulated and how conflicting changes resolve. Make shared commits explicit and show their scope. If a contact disappears, cancel or settle only the corresponding operation and leave independent work active.

## Accessibility

Evaluate reach from seated as well as standing positions. Keep local labels readable from their intended side and expose a way to orient shared information. Provide accessible companion controls when the installation's surface cannot support an essential task for some participants.

## Evidence

Microsoft's Surface tabletop guides cover simultaneous touch and orientation. The 2011 Surface guide discusses contact orientation and testing from different positions in its 360-degree design guidance. See the [archive map](aresluna-source-map.md).

## Validation

Have two people on different sides organize separate items, then attempt to move the same item and commit a shared change. Repeat from seated positions and use the tap path for one move. Observe reach, conflict handling, label orientation, and recovery after one participant cancels.
